What a Samhithe School Visit Looks Like

See how a Samhithe school visit unfolds: observation, practical workshops, and actionable strategies for Bengaluru teachers to support student well-being.

Nidhi, Samhithe · Oct 30, 2026 · 6 min read

The Reality of the Classroom

Walk into a typical classroom in Bengaluru, and you are likely to see a room full of energy, curiosity, and yes, some friction. A child might be struggling to transition from circle time to snack time, or perhaps a group is navigating a disagreement during free play. For teachers, academic coordinators, and principals, these moments are not just disruptions; they are data points. They are the moments where the gap between a child’s internal state and their external behaviour becomes visible.

Samhithe exists to help educators bridge that gap. We do not come in to judge the classroom environment or to suggest that teachers are doing something wrong. Instead, we come to equip them with a sharper lens. Our goal is to move from reactive management to proactive understanding, helping schools create spaces where every child, regardless of their neurotype or temperament, feels seen and supported. A school visit is not a lecture; it is a collaborative session designed to make the daily routine smoother for both the educator and the learner.

Step 1: The Quiet Observation

The visit begins not with a presentation, but with silence. Nidhi Rao, our founder and lead psychologist, spends the initial portion of the visit observing the classroom in action. This is not a performance review. It is a structured look at the ecology of the room. We watch how transitions happen, how instructions are given, and how children respond to sensory inputs like noise, light, or movement.

During this time, teachers are not expected to perform. They continue their natural rhythm. However, prior to the visit, teachers complete a short observation sheet. This tool helps us identify specific patterns—such as which times of day feel most chaotic or which children seem to withdraw or act out. By grounding our visit in what the teachers are already seeing, we ensure that the subsequent workshop is relevant to their actual daily reality, not a theoretical ideal.

Step 2: The ABC Lens and the Four Reasons

Once the observation phase is complete, we gather the teaching staff. The first major shift in perspective we introduce is the ABC lens: Antecedent, Behaviour, and Consequence. This simple framework helps teachers decode what is happening in a split second.

More importantly, we introduce the concept that all behaviour serves a purpose. Every action a child takes is an attempt to communicate a need. We explore the four reasons behind behaviour as a whole framework. By understanding that a child’s action is driven by a specific unmet need—whether it is for connection, regulation, autonomy, or skill—we can stop seeing the behaviour as "bad" and start seeing it as "information." This shift alone reduces frustration and opens the door to more compassionate, effective responses.

Recognising Patterns Without Labelling

Part of this workshop involves helping teachers recognise early signs of differences in processing. We discuss what it looks like when a child struggles with sensory integration, or when anxiety manifests as avoidance. We also touch upon early indicators that might suggest ADHD, not to diagnose, but to understand the neurological underpinnings of the behaviour. The focus remains on what the teacher can notice and how they can adjust their approach, rather than assigning medical labels. This empowers teachers to support the child in the moment, without needing to be a clinician.

Step 3: Connection Before Correction

The core of our training is the principle of "connection before the behaviour." Too often, we try to correct behaviour after it has escalated. Samshithe teaches that connection is the preventative measure. When a child feels regulated and connected, they are far more capable of following instructions and engaging with peers.

We demonstrate practical ways to build this connection. It is not about grand gestures. It is about small, consistent interactions. It is about reading the room before the meltdown happens. It is about co-regulation—using your own calm presence to help a dysregulated child find their footing. We show teachers how to model this calm, how to use their tone and body language to de-escalate tension, and how to be the anchor in the child’s storm.

Step 4: The Zero-Cost Toolkit

Theory is essential, but the classroom runs on practical tools. One of the most valuable parts of the visit is the introduction to a zero-cost toolkit. These are strategies that require no budget, only a shift in routine. We provide concrete examples of:

  • Visual Schedules: Helping children anticipate what comes next, reducing anxiety around transitions.
  • First–Then Boards: Clear, visual cues that guide behaviour without constant verbal reminders.
  • Choices of Two: Offering limited options to give children a sense of autonomy and control.
  • Social Stories: Simple narratives that explain social expectations and scenarios.
  • Calming Corners: Designating a safe space for regulation, distinct from punitive isolation.
  • Feelings Charts: Tools to help children identify and express their emotions.
  • Movement Breaks: Integrating physical activity to reset focus and energy levels.

These are not complex interventions. They are small tweaks to the environment that can have a profound impact on the classroom climate. We walk teachers through how to implement these immediately, using materials they already have or can easily create.

Step 5: Co-Regulation in the Moment

Even with the best preparation, meltdowns will happen. The workshop dedicates significant time to the "in the moment" response. We practice co-regulation techniques. This involves recognising the early signs of dysregulation and stepping in to help the child calm down, rather than demanding they comply. We discuss how to lower the stakes, how to use silence, and how to create a safe container for the child’s big emotions. This is not about giving in to demands; it is about building the child’s capacity to self-regulate over time by first helping them regulate with your support.

"We do not aim to eliminate behaviour. We aim to understand its language. When a child acts out, they are speaking. Our job is to learn how to listen, and then help them find a better way to speak."

What Happens Next: The School Partnership

A single visit is a spark, but lasting change requires a fire. The visit is the entry point to our longer-term School Partnership Program. After the initial workshop, schools have the option to deepen the work. This involves ongoing support, deeper dives into specific classroom challenges, and tailored workshops for staff who may need more intensive guidance. The partnership is flexible and responsive to the school’s evolving needs.

We help schools build a culture of inclusion that goes beyond the classroom walls. This includes strategies for parent communication during PTMs, where teachers can share observations in a way that is supportive and collaborative. It includes training for new staff and refresher sessions for veteran teachers. The goal is to create a sustainable ecosystem where well-being is embedded in the school’s DNA, not just an add-on.

Conclusion

A school visit from Samhithe is about practical empowerment. It is about giving teachers the confidence to handle the complex, messy, beautiful reality of child development. It is about moving from a place of exhaustion to a place of understanding. By focusing on the ABC lens, the four reasons behind behaviour, and a zero-cost toolkit of practical strategies, we help schools create environments where every child can thrive.

If you are a school leader in Bengaluru looking to enhance your school’s approach to child and family wellness, we invite you to explore this partnership. Let us bring the observation, the lens, and the tools to your campus.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to be a psychologist to participate in the school visit?

No, the visit is designed for principals, teachers, and academic coordinators. It focuses on practical, classroom-ready strategies that any educator can apply.

Will Samhithe diagnose any children during the visit?

No, Samhithe does not diagnose. We focus on helping teachers understand behaviour patterns and implement supportive strategies without labelling children.

How long does a typical school visit last?

The duration is flexible and tailored to the school’s schedule and needs. Specific timing is discussed during the initial arrangement.
