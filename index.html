Why Samhithe

When the adults feel capable, children feel secure.

Every child goes through moments that are hard to understand — a sudden tantrum, a refusal to go to school, big feelings that seem to come out of nowhere. These are a normal part of growing up. But in the moment, it's the parent or teacher right there who has to respond, often without knowing what will help.

Samhithe exists to support those adults. We help parents and teachers understand what a child is going through, and give them practical ways to respond — whether that means changing a routine, the words they use, or the environment around the child.

When the adults around a child feel calm, capable and on the same page, the child feels it too — and grows with more confidence at home and in school.

You don't have to figure it all out alone.

Samhithe may help if you're…

  • Finding it hard to help your child with big feelings
  • Struggling to set boundaries that actually hold
  • Stuck around routines, screens, food, sleep or sibling fights
  • Wondering why your child's behaviour has changed
  • A teacher looking for ways to support every child in your class
  • Simply wanting to understand your child better

You don't need a diagnosis — or a "big" problem — to seek support.

What we believe

The principles behind everything we do.

Change happens where the child lives.

Children learn and grow in everyday moments — at home and at school. So we work through the parents and teachers who are there for those moments, and help them respond consistently.

Behaviour is communication.

Every behaviour is a child trying to tell us something. We help parents and teachers see it differently — and discover what their child has been saying all along.

The whole child, not just the behaviour.

Emotions, boundaries, routines, the language we use, the environment we create and the way we see a child — we look at all of it, because every part shapes how a child grows.

Practical, never overwhelming.

A few realistic changes you can actually try — not a long list of rules to live up to.

No labels, no judgement.

Children are understood, not labelled. Parents are supported, not blamed. Most are doing far better than they think.

Together, always.

"Samhithe" means togetherness. Parents, teachers and professionals work best when they're on the same page.

How I work

Evidence-based at the core. Warm and playful in the room.

01

Behaviour science, applied with heart

My background is ABA-informed — grounded in the science of how behaviour is learned. But I adapt it to the child and family in front of me, never a rigid protocol.

02

Looking for the "why" first

Instead of only asking "how do we stop this?", I ask "what is this behaviour telling us?" Every plan starts with a few possible explanations, tested against real life at home or in class.

03

Learning through play and the senses

Experiential, activity-based and multisensory. Children learn through their bodies long before they learn through words.

04

Caring for the carer

A calm child needs a calm adult. I pay attention to how parents and teachers are doing too — their triggers, their tiredness, their own need for support.

05

No labels, no blame

Children are seen and understood — not labelled or sent away. Parents are met without judgement. Most are already doing far better than they think.

06

Something to take home

After a session, I send families a personal takeaway note: what we noticed, a gentle plan for the next two weeks, and a few do's and avoids.

Why "Samhithe"?

Samhithe comes from an idea of togetherness — of things joined as one. It sits at the heart of the practice: children don't grow in isolation. Parents, caregivers, teachers and professionals are all part of a child's world, and work best together.

Beyond the counselling room

I'm a trained classical dancer with Radhakalpa Dance Company and a theatre artist. It's why my work feels playful and expressive — children learn through their bodies long before they learn through words.

02

For parents · Ongoing

Parenting Guidance

Regular sessions that turn understanding into everyday change — building a trusting, consistent relationship with your child, so your guidance lands without shouting or force.

How it works

  1. Understand. We map the patterns using the ABC lens and ask: is this a skill your child hasn't learned yet, or one they can do but aren't using?
  2. Plan. Simple, home-friendly tools — a visual daily schedule, timers and warnings before endings, "first this, then that", choices of two, and small connection rituals like a bedtime story.
  3. Practise & adjust. You try it for two weeks, then we check in on what's working — for your child, and for you.

What we work on together

  • Transitions, meltdowns and calming down
  • Screen time, routines, mealtimes and bedtime
  • Boundaries that hold — "ignore the behaviour, not the child"
  • Your own triggers and regulation — your calm is their calm
  • Simple play-based and sensory activities to try with your child at home
  • With older children and pre-teens: leading with love, not control — and writing the rules together

Questions parents ask

Before you book.

Do I need a diagnosis to book?

No. You can come with a parenting concern, a behaviour you don't understand, a developmental question, or simply a feeling that you're stuck.

How do I know if my child's behaviour is "normal"?

Many behaviours — tantrums, testing limits, clinginess — are a normal part of growing up. What matters is how often they happen, how intense they are, and how much they get in the way of daily life. If you're wondering, that's reason enough to talk.

Where should I start — Consultation or Guidance?

Most families start with a Parent Consultation. By the end of it, you and Nidhi will have a clear sense of whether ongoing Parenting Guidance would help.

Do you work directly with children?

Individual sessions are mainly with parents and caregivers — the adults who are with a child every day are where lasting change happens. If it would help to meet your child, Nidhi will let you know.

Is this therapy?

Parent consultations are supportive, guidance-focused sessions to understand your child's needs and find practical next steps. They aren't a substitute for formal psychological or psychiatric treatment when that's needed.

Do you diagnose ADHD or autism?

No. Samhithe doesn't provide formal diagnosis or diagnostic assessments. When an assessment may help, Nidhi will guide you towards the right professional.

Are sessions online?

Yes — individual sessions are 60 minutes, online on Google Meet. Workshops are held in person at your school or venue.

How do I pay, and what if I need to reschedule?

You pay ₹1,000 by UPI when you book and receive a Google Meet link by email straight away. To reschedule, simply reply to your confirmation email.

Is what I share kept confidential?

Yes. What you share stays between you and Nidhi, except in the rare case where a child's safety is at risk.
